Connect an AI assistant

To check what AI assistants say about you, this page needs to be able to ask them. That takes an API key — a password from the AI company that lets this page send questions on your behalf. You pay them directly for what you use.

New to this? Here is the whole idea. More and more people ask ChatGPT or Claude "what's the best tool for X" instead of searching. You cannot see those answers, so you cannot tell whether you get mentioned, whether a rival gets recommended instead, or whether what is said about you is even true. This tool asks those questions for you, on a schedule, and keeps score.
Why some are marked "actions only". Claude and Gemini can be used straight from this page — add a key and they work. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews block requests coming from a web page, so they can only run on a server. There is a way to do that further on, but you do not need it to start. One key for Claude is enough.

Or try it without keys

Just want a look around first?

Demo mode makes up a company, a set of questions and a batch of fake answers so you can walk the whole thing end to end. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing costs you anything. Turn it off when you want real numbers.

Which website are we checking?

Enter your domain. The assistant reads your site and works out what you sell, who you compete with, and which topics your buyers care about. You get to correct all of it on the next screen, so a rough first pass is fine.

Only needed if you sell several different things. A bank that offers cards, loans and savings should say which one — otherwise you may end up measuring the wrong part of the business.

Check what it found

Worth a minute of your time. Everything from here on is built on what is on this screen — especially the competitor list. If a real rival is missing, you will never find out when an assistant recommends them instead of you.

Competitors

Add anyone a buyer might pick instead of you. If one of these is a brand you own, tick "sibling" — it is not a rival, but it is still worth watching, because assistants often mix up a parent company with the brands it owns.

Topics

The subjects your buyers care about — "pricing", "ease of use", "customer support". Questions get grouped under these so you can see which subjects you show up for and which you are invisible on. Keep each to two or three words, or the labels get cut off in the charts.

What is actually true about you?

Optional, but it is the most useful five minutes here. Assistants often repeat things about companies that are out of date or simply wrong. List the real facts — prices, limits, what is included — and every answer gets checked against your list.

Why bother. "An assistant says bad things about us" is an opinion, and someone will argue with it. "An assistant told people our fee is 2.9% when we cut it to 2.59% in March, and it was reading a page from 2023" is a fact, and someone can go fix it.
Write plain statements, and add a date where it matters. Only direct contradictions get flagged — anything your list does not mention is left alone. A short, accurate list is better than a long, vague one. You can always add more later.
Leave it empty to skip — you can come back and add facts any time.

The questions we will ask

These are sent to the assistants word for word, the same way every time. About 6 in 10 deliberately do not mention your brand — those are the ones that tell you whether you come up at all when someone asks about the category rather than about you.

Read them now, change them rarely. Comparing one month to the next only works if the questions stayed the same. Edit anything that sounds wrong before your first run; after that, leave them be.

Run the check

This asks every question and records what comes back. One run is a snapshot of where you stand today; run it again next week or next month and you start to see which way you are moving. Results are saved as they arrive, so closing the tab only loses the run in progress, never what you already collected.

Why ask the same question more than once. Assistants do not give the same answer every time. Ask three times and you might be mentioned twice and missed once — that is a very different position from being mentioned every time, and asking once cannot tell them apart. Three is the smallest number that shows you the difference. Start small: 5 questions, 1 time each, checking off — enough to see it work for a few cents.
